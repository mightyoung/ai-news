## 2. Marco Pavone（@drmapavone）· 2026-10-21 04:06 CST

> Do Vision-Language-Action models mean what they say?
>
> Embodied chain-of-thought offers a promising way to leverage additional inference-time computation for robot policies, with empirical evidence that it can improve downstream task performance. However, in practice the extent to which this chain-of-thought actually influences a robot policy’s chosen actions is not fully understood yet.
>
> In our new paper on #embodied #reasoning, we formally define the notion of faithfulness i.e., whether the chain-of-thought truthfully reflects the policy's decision for #Physical #AI and argue that the faithfulness of robot policies should play an explicit role in algorithm design.
>
> We train a critic of embodied faithfulness, identifying the signatures of inconsistent and incoherent reasoning, and use it as a reward signal during reinforcement learning (RL) post-training of a robot policy. We call our critic Pinocchio 🤥: a model that learns to tell when a robot policy is 'lying' about the justifications for a decision. Pinocchio scores two complementary failure modes: whether the reasoning is grounded in what the robot actually sees, and whether it is coherent step-to-step through the chain-of-thought.
>
> We find that post-training for faithfulness yields a +18% improvement in reasoning consistency over standard baselines, and critically, makes the policy 1.6× more responsive to rare, out-of-distribution scenarios that confuse SoTA models.
>
> These findings suggest that faithful embodied reasoning is not merely a post-hoc interpretability property, but an important ingredient for building more robust and trustworthy autonomous systems.
>
> 📄 Paper: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.04681
> 🌐 Project page: https://mjf-su.github.io/pinocchio/
> 💻 Code: Coming soon!
>
> Great work led by Matthew Foutter, in collaboration with Matteo Cercola Lena Wild Yunshan Wang Michelle Li and @DanieleGammelli 
>
