## 2. Dr. Cheems Wang 🏡（@AlbertW24045555）· 2026-10-19 10:47 CST

> (1/5) New preprint: TreeOPSD, critic-free RL for long-horizon LLM agents 🌳
>
> Long-horizon agents receive a single outcome reward after many turns, and each rollout batch feeds several sequential mini-batch updates while the policy drifts from the one that sampled it.
>
> TreeOPSD backs terminal rewards up a rollout tree and splits each update into two steps: construct a target for every turn, then match the current policy to it with forward KL.
>
> This leads to:
> • closed-form, bounded turn targets that commute with tree backup, so shared prefixes are trained once with no loss of credit
> • the exact tree policy gradient on the first mini-batch, and updates that stop at the target and reverse after overshoot
> • consistent gains over GRPO, GSPO and a matched tree baseline, largest on the longest-horizon tasks
>
> On Deep Research, TreeOPSD lifts Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 from 16.23 (GRPO) to 21.20 average accuracy.
>
> Title: Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-Horizon Agentic Reinforcement Learning
> Paper: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23181
> Together with @TencentHunyuan
>

## 3. Dr. Cheems Wang 🏡（@AlbertW24045555）· 2026-10-19 10:47 CST · 相关

> (2/5) Key idea 1: a closed-form target that is consistent with the tree.
>
> A KL-regularized proximal step maps each turn's backed-up credit V to a target for how much more or less likely that turn should become than under the sampling policy, written as a Bernoulli probability. The solution is affine, q = 1/2 + V/τ, and calibrating τ from the group size keeps every target within a fixed radius at any depth, without clipping.
>
> Being affine, the map commutes with tree backup: a parent's target is exactly the weighted average of its children's. Matching one target per shared turn therefore gives the same gradient as aggregating all the leaves below it. A logistic target breaks this property and trains worse.

## 4. Dr. Cheems Wang 🏡（@AlbertW24045555）· 2026-10-19 10:47 CST · 相关

> (3/5) Key idea 2: updates that know where to stop.
> Every mini-batch matches the current policy to these frozen targets with forward KL. The resulting weight on each turn is τ(q − p), with p the policy's current probability for that turn. On the first mini-batch p = 1/2, so the update equals the tree policy gradient. 
>
> Later, the weight shrinks as the policy approaches the target, vanishes at it, and reverses after overshoot, while a fixed-credit policy gradient keeps pushing with its collection-time weight.
>
> The correction stays bounded, so it avoids the variance of importance ratios, and every decrease in the loss improves the proximal RL objective by the same amount.

## 5. Dr. Cheems Wang 🏡（@AlbertW24045555）· 2026-10-19 10:47 CST · 相关

> Empirically, TreeOPSD gives the best average on Multi-Hop QA and Deep Research with both Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-30B-A3B. The closest control is TreePO, which shares the trees, credits and optimizer blocks but keeps a fixed credit coefficient; on Deep Research (BrowseComp-Plus, BrowseComp, GAIA, xbench-DeepSearch), TreeOPSD improves over it from 13.07 to 15.45 (4B) and from 17.59 to 21.20 (30B).
>
> The trained agents also take more interaction turns and stay ahead of GRPO at equal training time. The gains hold on Qwen3-1.7B, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and across five seeds.

## 6. Dr. Cheems Wang 🏡（@AlbertW24045555）· 2026-10-19 10:47 CST · 相关

> (5/5) More broadly, TreeOPSD carries the target-projection view of RLVR from our LPO work (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06139) over to multi-turn agents. 
>
> Instead of a listwise distribution over a few sampled siblings, each turn receives its own behavior-relative Bernoulli target, so several useful continuations can rise together, which a sibling-normalized update cannot do.
>
> Because TreeOPSD only decides how an acquired rollout tree updates the policy, it composes directly with rollout-allocation methods that decide where to branch, such as TRACE.
